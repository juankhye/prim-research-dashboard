First principles

What does Primoris actually do?

Forget the ticker. Imagine you're the head of ERCOT (Texas's grid) and you need a new high-voltage transmission line built across 80 miles of West Texas. Or you're Hecate Energy and you need someone to engineer, procure, and construct a 500 MW solar farm. You don't have those skills in-house. You hire a specialty contractor. That's Primoris.

Founded

Founded as ARB, Inc. in Bakersfield, CA in 1960. Reorganized as Primoris (NV) in 2003. Public on NYSE since 2008 (ticker PRIM).

Headquarters

Dallas, TX — 2300 N. Field St, Suite 1900. Operations across US + Canada. ENR rank: #4 specialty contractor.

People & Iron

~18,500 employees (3,055 salaried + 15,471 hourly). Fleet: 8,000+ vehicles & 25,000 assets. ~30% hourly are unionized.

The two segments — what each builds

Utilities (34% of revenue, 2025) — the steady half

Long-term Master Service Agreements (MSAs) with vertically integrated utilities. Recurring, unit-priced work that gets renewed year after year. Top customers: Oncor (Texas), Xcel Energy, Pacific Gas & Electric, Southern California Gas, Duke Energy, Sempra, Consumers Energy, Dominion, Florida Power & Light.

  • Gas distribution & utility services — burying gas mains, leak repair, metering ($941M revenue '26E)
  • Electric transmission & distribution (T&D) — power lines, substations, grid hardening ($945M '26E)
  • Telecom / fiber — burying fiber, 5G tower work ($347M '26E)
  • PLH Group — power delivery (acquired 2022, $522M '26E)
Energy (66% of revenue, 2025) — the lumpy half

Big fixed-price EPC projects: engineering, procurement, construction. Bigger margins on big wins, but also where this quarter blew up. Top customers: Hecate Energy, Intersect Power, Avantus, D.E. Shaw Renewables, Williams, Valero, ExxonMobil, Enterprise Pipeline, Texas DOT.

  • Renewables / utility-scale solar — #2 EPC in US (3.4 GW installed 2024); 90%+ self-perform
  • Pipeline — large-diameter natural gas pipelines (the cyclical one)
  • Industrial / Gas Generation — simple-cycle peaker plants (the next leg of growth)
  • Heavy civil — DOT roadwork, TX & LA
  • Premier PV — proprietary eBOS manufacturing (Dallas, scaling 1.5GW→4.5GW)
  • PayneCrest Electric (acquired May '26) — data center interior electrical
Business model

Why this business is good — and why the same thing makes it dangerous

The thing that makes Primoris's economics work — they self-perform 75-80% of every job — is also the thing that makes them blow up when projects go wrong. There's no subcontractor to absorb the loss. When the job runs hot, Primoris eats the cost overrun. Q1 2026 was a textbook example.

The "self-perform" moat

~75–80% of work done by their own employees and equipment (vs. subcontracting). In the Renewables sub-segment that figure rises to 90%+. Like a restaurant that grows its own vegetables and butchers its own meat — more control, more margin, but no one else to blame when the kitchen fire happens.

Backlog quality — not all backlog is equal

Of $11.9B total backlog at YE 2025, roughly $7.5B (65%) is recurring MSA work with utility customers — predictable, sticky, low-margin. The other $4.4B is fixed-price project work — bigger margins but binary outcomes.

Multi-year framework contracts where the utility commits to using Primoris for ongoing maintenance and small projects. Like a subscription. Provides predictable recurring revenue.

EPC 101

The 8 terms you need to read this stock

Specialty infrastructure has its own jargon. Here's the cheat sheet — you can scan this once and never need to Google again.

EPC — Engineering, Procurement, Construction
One contractor handles the entire job: designs it (E), buys the equipment & materials (P), builds it (C). Single point of accountability. Primoris is an EPC.
Self-perform
Doing work with your own employees & equipment instead of subcontracting. Higher control, higher margins on a good day, higher risk on a bad day.
MSA — Master Service Agreement
Multi-year framework contract with a utility for ongoing maintenance and small projects. Predictable recurring revenue. ~50% of PRIM backlog.
Backlog
Total dollar value of awarded work not yet built. $11.9B for PRIM = ~1.5 years of revenue visibility.
eBOS — electrical Balance of System
Everything in a solar farm except the panels and inverters: combiner boxes, disconnects, wire harnesses. PRIM has its own brand: Premier PV, made in Dallas. Scaling from 1.5GW→4.5GW capacity.
Peaker plant / Simple-cycle CT
Natural gas turbine that fires up only when grid demand peaks (a few hours/day). Quick to build, high-margin construction. PRIM's next big growth lever — $6B opportunity funnel.
T&D / Power Delivery
Transmission (high-voltage long-distance lines) & Distribution (low-voltage local). PRIM is shifting mix from 80/20 to 60/40 — more transmission = higher margin.
OBBBA / IRA
One Big Beautiful Bill Act / Inflation Reduction Act — federal tax credits driving solar EPC demand. Sunset risk post-2030 is the bear case anchor.
EBITDA
Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, Amortization. Approximation of operating cash earnings. PRIM 2026E: ~$490M (cut from $570M).
EV/EBITDA
Enterprise Value ÷ EBITDA. The standard valuation multiple for E&C. PRIM trades 13.1x 2027E vs peers at 17.8x — a ~25% discount.

Revenue & cost drivers

The plumbing — what actually moves the P&L

In an EPC contractor, revenue is driven by backlog conversion (how fast orders become billed work) and book-to-bill (whether new wins replace burned backlog). Costs are dominated by craft labor (~50% of project cost) and materials/equipment (~40%). Margin is whatever is left after weather, soil conditions, and scope creep.

The Q1 2026 disaster

Anatomy of a 50% drop — what actually went wrong

May 5, 2026. Primoris reports Q1. Adj. EBITDA misses by 35%. The 2026 guide gets cut by 14%. The Energy margin range is permanently lowered. The stock falls 50% in one day — wiping $5B of market cap. This is what happened.

01
Six solar projects went sideways simultaneously

All booked in 2H24, in geographies where PRIM didn't have a deep operational track record (Indiana, Northeast). Cost overruns of $35-40M booked in Q1 alone.

02
Subsurface conditions — rock & agricultural drain tiles

Indiana sites had unforeseen underground rock and drainage infrastructure that wasn't in the bid. Each pile drive and each redesign added cost.

03
Northeast weather + labor productivity issues

Inclement weather, mismanaged crew sequencing across projects, productivity well below bid assumptions.

04
Project pushouts — $400M of solar revenue moved out of 2026

Customers delaying financial close on new projects → revenue moved to 2027. Adds another $45M EBITDA hit to '26 from underutilized labor & equipment.

05
Margin range permanently lowered (the structural hit)

Energy segment target margin band: 10–12% → 9–11%. Bulls say this is just management being conservative; bears say it's an admission that the prior range was unsustainable.

06
Hagens Berman investigation launched

Plaintiff's law firm launched investigation into whether prior public statements obscured the true health of the renewables portfolio (Q4 2025 had similar — but smaller — issues management called "isolated").

The reset arithmetic — $80M EBITDA gone

Original 2026 guide: $570M midpoint. New guide: $490M. The $80M cut breaks down: +$30M from PayneCrest acquisition, −$35-40M Q1 cost overruns, −$45M revenue pushout, −$25M lower margins on completing problem projects.

End markets & megatrends

The three forces that justify owning this name

The bull case isn't built on solar — it's built on three secular tailwinds independent of solar. AI-driven data center load growth, gas-gen super-cycle, and grid modernization are each a multi-billion-dollar TAM expansion. Primoris is positioned in all three.

⚡
Data center buildout (AI super-cycle)

Hyperscaler capex driving multi-year demand for site prep, power generation, grid interconnection, and now "inside the box" electrical work — interior wiring of the data hall itself. This is exactly what PayneCrest brought.

PayneCrest = ~40% data center exposure. PRIM did $850M of "outside-the-box" data-center enabling work in 2025.
🔌
Grid modernization (transmission super-cycle)

Top 10 US utilities project ~$145B of T&D capex over 2026-2028. PRIM is intentionally shifting its mix from 80/20 distribution-to-transmission toward 60/40 — higher complexity, higher margins.

SNL: total US utility capex $116B (2018) → $251B (2026E). Electric utility capex 2x'd.
🔥
Gas generation super-cycle (peakers)

Data centers + AI need dispatchable power — solar/wind aren't always-on. Simple-cycle natural gas peakers are the answer. PRIM is one of few EPCs with the niche expertise. $6B opportunity funnel.

$1.5-2.0B near-term funnel; $800M of "verbal" awards expected to firm up in 2H 2026 (key catalyst).

The renewables outlook — no longer the growth engine

Per Jefferies post-Q1: PRIM has stopped pursuing the geographies that caused the trouble. Renewables now projected to peak at $2.7B in 2028 (from $3.0B in 2025), down from prior $3.1B model. The OBBBA tax-credit cliff post-2030 caps long-term solar growth. Gas gen is the replacement story. Battery storage is a smaller adjacency (~$250M in 2025).

The strategic logic

Before PayneCrest, PRIM could do site prep, build the substation, and run transmission to the data center — but had to subcontract the interior wiring. After PayneCrest, PRIM can pitch "true turnkey" for hyperscaler campuses. One contract, one accountability point, no margin leak to subs.

Beyond data centers, PayneCrest also services aerospace, pharmaceuticals, and advanced manufacturing — broadens the TAM. Bridges electrical capabilities across PRIM's Renewables, Power, and Industrial offerings.

Past PRIM acquisitions: PLH Group (2022, $470M, power delivery), Future Infrastructure (2021, $620M, comms), Willbros (2018, $138M, oil & gas pipelines). PayneCrest is the largest data-center-focused deal.

Valuation

Trading at a 30%+ discount to peers — but with reason

Specialty E&C peers (Quanta, MasTec, EMCOR, Dycom, MYR Group) trade at 17-27x 2026E EV/EBITDA. PRIM trades at 13-15x. The discount is real, but so are the reasons: lower margins, customer concentration, recent execution misses. The bull case is multiple expansion as PRIM proves out its mix shift.

2026E EV/EBITDA — peer comp

PRIM at 15.3x is 4.7 turns below peer average of 20x. Even matching peers would deliver ~30% upside before any earnings improvement.

Margin & ROIC vs peers

PRIM's 11.3% ROIC is best-in-class — higher than PWR (8.9%) or MTZ (9.2%) — but EBITDA margin (6.6%) is lowest of the three. Roth's bull thesis hangs on this ROIC.

Key risks

What could break this story — ranked by severity

The unwind risk in PRIM is not the macro economy — it's idiosyncratic execution. Eight specific risks are well-flagged in the broker reports. Severity rated 1-5 below.

Fixed-price contract execution

EPC takes all cost-overrun risk. Q1 2026 demonstrated this. Cannot be eliminated structurally — labor productivity, subsurface conditions, weather are inherent.

Customer concentration

Top 10 customers = 41-53% of revenue. MSAs have no minimum purchase. A single hyperscaler/utility pull-back can swing a quarter.

Executive turnover / talent leakage

Former CEO McCormick out March '25. New CEO Vadlamudi (ex-Jacobs) installed Nov '25. VPs left for Pike, MYR, Ferreira. Per Expert Calls: lost contracts in Texas as a direct result.

Craft labor scarcity

Aging utility workforce + AI-driven demand surge. Linemen, electricians, welders — all in short supply. Forces premium wages, which compress margins on legacy bids.

OBBBA / IRA tax credit modifications

Aggressive modifications could derail developer capex. PRIM contracts have inflation pass-through but not policy-shock pass-through. Solar specifically vulnerable.

PayneCrest integration

Reference

Quick-reference glossary

Bookmark this section. These are the terms you'll see in every PRIM call, broker report, and 10-K — and that you need to fluently hold in your head.

Backlog
Awarded but un-built work. PRIM Dec 2025: $11.9B. Includes both fixed-price (binding) and N12M MSA (recurring framework agreements).
Book-to-bill
(New orders ÷ revenue burned) for a period. >1.0x = backlog growing. PRIM Q4 25 Energy: 1.9x. Q1 26: 0.2x.
Burn rate
Revenue recognized per quarter from backlog. Implies how long current backlog will last (PRIM ~1.5 years).
EPC
Engineering, Procurement, Construction. Single contractor handles all three phases. PRIM is one of largest US EPCs.
eBOS
Electrical Balance of System — solar farm components excluding panels & inverters. PRIM brand: Premier PV.
ERCOT
Electric Reliability Council of Texas — TX's power grid operator. Major source of T&D demand.
FERC
Federal Energy Regulatory Commission. Controls interstate electric & gas regulation.
Fixed-price contract
EPC quotes one price; eats overruns, keeps savings. High-risk/high-reward. PRIM ~35% of backlog.
Hyperscaler
Mega data center operators (AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle). Largest source of new electrical / cooling / fiber demand.
IRA
Inflation Reduction Act 2022. Tax credits for solar/storage. Driving force of solar EPC growth — sunset risk post-2030.
MSA
Master Service Agreement. Multi-year framework with utility. Recurring, lower-margin, high-renewal-rate work.
OBBBA
"One Big Beautiful Bill Act" — successor framework expected to extend / restructure post-IRA tax credits.
Peaker plant
Simple-cycle gas turbine that runs only at grid demand peaks. Quick to deploy, high-margin EPC. Gas-gen super-cycle.
PJM
Mid-Atlantic regional grid (PA, NJ, MD, VA, etc.). Major data center / gas-gen demand zone.
Power Delivery
T&D + substations + grid infrastructure. PRIM's intentional mix shift: 80/20 distribution-to-transmission → 60/40 (higher margin).
Self-perform
Doing work with own employees / equipment vs. subcontracting. PRIM 75-80% overall, 90%+ in renewables.
Substantial completion
Project milestone where principal performance is done; turn over to customer. Most cost overruns happen before this milestone.
T&D
Transmission & Distribution. High-voltage long-distance lines + low-voltage local feeders.
Unit-price contract
Per-unit pricing for repeated tasks (vs. fixed-price for whole project). Lower risk, lower margin. ~60% of utility segment.
